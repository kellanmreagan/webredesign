❄️ Winter in the Western U.P. ❄️

When the snow falls, the Upper Peninsula transforms into a true winter playground. From snowshoeing and cross-country skiing through the quiet beauty of the Ottawa National Forest, to downhill skiing, sledding, and tubing at nearby hills, there's no shortage of ways to enjoy the season. Add in ice fishing, snowmobiling, and the famous Michigan Tech Winter Carnival, and you'll see why the U.P. is one of the Midwest's favorite winter destinations.

XC Skiing / Snowshoeing / Winter Biking

Check ahead to make sure the area you are going to allows your activity on the trails.

The Ottawa National Forest offers a winter wonderland for snowshoe enthusiasts. Encompassing nearly one million acres, there is sure to be something for everyone in the beautiful snow-covered landscape. There are no specific trails designated for snowshoeing — with a compass and map, a person can go virtually anywhere in the Forest.

Ice Fishing

The state boasts more than 150 species of fish and almost all of them can be caught through the ice. Popular species when the ice freezes over include panfish such as bluegill and perch as well as northern pike, walleye and various trout species.

Michigan Technological University (MTU) Winter Carnival

For one week in February, the Michigan Tech campus celebrates winter in the Upper Peninsula. Organized by Blue Key National Honor Society since 1934, Winter Carnival started in 1922 and has grown to become one of the largest annual winter festivals in the nation — featuring dozens of huge, intricate snow statues, broomball, comedy skits, sleigh rides, a queen coronation, and lots of winter fun. The statue “all-nighter” is one night not to miss — the last night for statues to be built, and the best night to see all the engineering that goes into massive, award-winning statues.
