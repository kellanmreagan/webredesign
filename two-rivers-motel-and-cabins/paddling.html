Paddling Adventures in Michigan's Western Upper Peninsula

The Western Upper Peninsula is home to some of Michigan's most scenic paddling opportunities. From the crystal-clear lakes of Sylvania Wilderness to the winding rivers of the Ottawa National Forest, paddlers of all experience levels can find an adventure that fits their style.

Why Paddle the Western U.P.?

The Western U.P. offers a unique paddling experience that combines remote wilderness, abundant wildlife, and uncrowded waterways. Visitors can enjoy scenic river floats, quiet wilderness lakes, canoe and kayak fishing, wildlife viewing, photography opportunities, fall color paddling, family-friendly outings, and advanced river adventures.

Featured Paddling Destinations

Sylvania Wilderness Canoe Area

One of the Midwest's premier paddling destinations, featuring dozens of interconnected lakes surrounded by old-growth forest. Clear water and quiet shorelines make this a favorite for families and photographers.

Best for: canoeing, kayaking, wildlife viewing, photography, beginner & intermediate paddlers

Cisco Chain of Lakes

Miles of interconnected lakes along the Michigan–Wisconsin border. Paddlers can explore multiple lakes in a single day while enjoying excellent fishing and scenic shorelines.

Best for: recreational kayaking, canoeing, fishing, full-day trips

Bond Falls Flowage

While many visitors know Bond Falls, fewer discover the calm waters of the flowage — a relaxing paddle with beautiful scenery and great wildlife viewing.

Best for: families, beginner paddlers, paddleboards, sunset paddles

Before You Go

Safety Tips

  • Wear a U.S. Coast Guard-approved life jacket whenever kayaking, canoeing, or stand-up paddleboarding
  • Check weather forecasts before launching and be prepared for changing conditions
  • Verify river and lake conditions before your trip
  • Bring drinking water, snacks, and a dry bag for important items
  • Carry a charged cell phone whenever possible
  • Dress appropriately for water temperatures, not just air temperatures

Paddling Solo?

  • Always wear your life jacket
  • Tell someone (could even be us!) where you're going
  • Let them know your planned route
  • Share your expected return time
  • Schedule a check-in time after your trip

Know Before You Go

  • Confirm access points and parking areas before leaving
  • Plan transportation if your trip uses separate launch and take-out locations
  • Paddle within your skill level
  • Be especially cautious on rivers with rapids, changing water levels, or strong currents
