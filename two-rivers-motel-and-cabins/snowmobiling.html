🌟 Off-Trail Riding Adventures

The U.S. Forest Service allows snowmobiling on any unplowed forest road and in all public areas in the Ottawa National Forest. Whether you're a novice rider curious about a short side trip, or an experienced sledder chasing deep snow, the opportunities are endless.

  • Ride with a buddy and let someone know your route
  • Carry GPS or a trail app and download the trails/roads to your device (cell service can be spotty)
  • Pack extra clothing, food, and emergency gear
  • Respect private property—stay within public land
  • Be mindful of changing weather and snow depth

Off-trail riding is at your own risk. Conditions can change quickly—ride smart and ride safe.

📡 Grooming Clubs & Trail Reports

Our trails are cared for by dedicated local snowmobile clubs — Sno Valley Riders (Trail 8 Groomers), Iron Range Trail Club, Baraga County Convention & Visitors Bureau, Gogebic Range Trail Authority, North Country Snowmobile Club UP, Keweenaw Trail Reports, and MISORVA Trail Reports.

🛡️ Safety & Property Etiquette

  • Always ride with a partner and let someone know your plan
  • Carry GPS/phone, spare gloves, tow strap, first-aid kit, and basic repair tools
  • Lake-effect snow can change fast — watch weather conditions
  • Stay on marked trails unless riding approved public land. Ride Right, Lead Right — groomers always have the right of way
  • Respect quiet hours at the property: 11pm–7am

❓ Snowmobiling FAQs

Can we ride from the property?

Yes — Trail #8 runs directly in front of Two Rivers Motel & Cabins, giving guests convenient ride-in / ride-out access to Michigan's Western U.P. snowmobile trail system. Unload your sled, gear up, and head straight to the trails without trailering each day.

When is snowmobile season?

Michigan snowmobile trails are officially open December 1 through March 31, weather permitting. January and February typically offer the most consistent riding conditions in the Western U.P.

Do you provide trail maps?

Yes — trail maps are available in our office. Each group receives one complimentary map during their stay, and additional maps are available for purchase.

Is off-trail riding allowed?

In many areas of the Ottawa National Forest, snowmobiling is allowed on unplowed Forest Service roads and approved public lands. Always verify current regulations and respect private property boundaries.

Where can I check grooming conditions?

Trail conditions are typically posted by local snowmobile clubs — Sno Valley Riders, Keweenaw Trail Reports, MISORVA trail reports, and other local clubs throughout the Western U.P.

Is there trailer parking available?

Yes — we offer plenty of trailer parking for snowmobile trailers of various sizes, with easy access to Trail #8 directly from the property.

What should we bring on a snowmobile trip?

Warm layered riding gear, spare gloves and socks, GPS or trail map apps, basic repair tools, snacks and water, and extra fuel or oil if needed.

Are the trails groomed regularly?

Yes — many trails in the Western Upper Peninsula are groomed regularly by dedicated local snowmobile clubs and volunteers. Remember: Ride Right, Lead Right — groomers always have the right of way.

Why stay in the Western U.P. for snowmobiling?

Heavy lake-effect snowfall, scenic forest riding, hundreds of miles of connected trails, access to the Ottawa National Forest, and small-town U.P. hospitality — the area around Kenton, Trout Creek, and Sidnaw offers some of the most scenic and adventurous snowmobiling in the Midwest.
