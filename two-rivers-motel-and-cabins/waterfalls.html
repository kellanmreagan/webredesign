Chasing Waterfalls in Michigan's Western Upper Peninsula

You can find tons of waterfalls in the surrounding areas. Agate Falls, Jumbo Falls, and Bond Falls are each only a few miles away from Two Rivers, but there are countless others. We believe that spring is the best time to view the waterfalls—the winter snow melt makes the waterfalls very active and strong.

Winter Info: please wear appropriate footwear—most waterfall areas will be ice covered and slippery. Be careful and respect posted signs.

Check out the Recreational Opportunity Guide to Ottawa Waterfalls PDF from the USDA, and the Lake Superior Circle Tour — Waterfalls guide.

Local Waterfalls

Bond Falls

A total drop of approximately 50 feet over fractured rock. Like the nearby Agate Falls, it is a Michigan State Scenic Site and flows down the Middle Branch of the Ontonagon River. Free parking, a picnic area, and an accessible boardwalk with six viewing platforms.

Jumbo Falls

Surrounded by huge cedars, a short but scenic waterfall. From Two Rivers, go straight across M-28 onto Golden Glow Road for 1.8 miles, turn left at the first intersection, take the right fork at .3 miles through an old sand pit, and park at the trout sign at .9 miles — follow the path upstream on foot.

Agate Falls

An official Michigan State Scenic Site on the Middle Branch of the Ontonagon River, 7 miles southeast of Bruce Crossing. Flows 39 feet over a shelf of sandstone; view via a railroad bridge or a short trail. Bond Falls is a mere 12-mile drive down river.

O Kun de Kun Falls

One of the most impressive plunging waterfalls in the U.P. The North Country Trail runs along the Baltimore River here with a long suspension footbridge. Trailhead on the east side of US-45, 7.8 miles north of Bruce Crossing — a moderately simple 1.5 mile trek to the suspension footbridge.

Duppy Falls

Three small drops in a quick row right before Jumbo River flows out of Iron County, about 5.1 miles south of Kenton just off NF-16, over tough weathered volcanic rock.

Canyon Falls

Trailhead on Sturgeon River at a roadside park along US-41 with picnic tables and restrooms. An easy 10-minute walk to a scenic box canyon and waterfalls, sometimes called the “Grand Canyon of Michigan.”

Sturgeon Falls

Located in the Sturgeon River Gorge Wilderness in Ottawa National Forest, a 300-foot-deep gorge. The drop is 25 feet but powerful. From Two Rivers, go east on M-28 to Sidnaw, turn north on gravel road 2200 past the Sidnaw store, then left onto 193 past the Bears Den Outlook — a 3/4 mile trail with a steep descent, wild and undeveloped.

Victoria Falls

Controlled by the amount of water flowing over Victoria Dam — may not be visible in drier times. Take Victoria Dam Rd West from Rockland about five miles, bearing left at the junction by the old village.

Bonanza Falls

Near Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park, close to Silver City. About 100 feet wide with multiple short drops over slate rock on the Big Iron River — an absolute must see.

Black Slate Falls, Quartzite Falls & Slate River Falls

Several drops along the scenic Slate River near L'Anse, reached via short easy trails through cedar forest. Slate River Falls is the highest at 25 feet.

Powerhouse Falls

In L'Anse, next to a decommissioned hydraulic powerhouse — a 15-foot drop over slate rock ledges. Explore upstream for Power Dam Falls and the remnants of an old removed dam.

Cascade Falls

A moderately difficult ~2 mile loop near Bergland via the Bluff Trail (challenging, great views) or the easier Main Trail. Leashed dogs allowed.

Greenstone Falls

In Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park on the Little Carp River, via a two-mile round trip off South Boundary Road with narrow planked boardwalks. The Greenstone Falls Cabin nearby can be rented overnight.

Root Beer Falls

Flows down Planter Creek near Wakefield — an 8-foot waterfall named for its rich brown tannin water creating a bubbly froth.

Shining Cloud Falls

A back-country waterfall in Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park dropping ~20 feet and splitting in two. Accessed via the Big Carp River Trail, about 8.5 miles from the Lake of the Clouds Scenic Overlook.

Presque Isle River Waterfalls Loop Trail

Just over 2 miles, near Wakefield along the western edge of Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park, with boardwalks, stairs, and a suspension bridge. Ample parking at the Presque Isle Scenic Area.

  • Manabezho Falls — named for an Ojibway god; 25 feet tall and 150 feet wide, the largest drop on the river, viewable from either side.
  • Manido Falls — Ojibway for “spirit”; the smallest on the trail at ~15 feet, with a crest between 50–150 feet depending on flow.
  • Nawadaha Falls — Ojibway for “in the midst of rapids”; a 15-foot drop with a crest up to 150 feet in spring.

Black River Waterfalls Trails

North of Bessemer within the Ottawa National Forest, five waterfalls not to be missed:

  • Great Conglomerate — ~3/4 mile from the Potawatomi/Gorge Falls lot; stairs and observation platforms overlook this 30-foot waterfall.
  • Potawatomi Falls — under a 1/4 mile trail; a 30-foot drop, 130 feet wide, and wheelchair accessible.
  • Gorge Falls — just downstream from Potawatomi Falls; a 25-foot waterfall in a deep narrow gorge.
  • Sandstone Falls — a short, steep ~1/4 mile hike with stairs; 15 feet tall and 30 feet wide.
  • Rainbow Falls — a 40-foot drop just before the Black River meets Lake Superior; reached from the Black River Harbor lot across the suspension bridge, or via the Rainbow Falls lot and a 3/4 mile trail to a viewing platform.
